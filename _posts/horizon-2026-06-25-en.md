This breakthrough allows historians and archaeologists to access and analyze previously unreadable ancient texts, potentially revealing new insights into the past and enhancing our understanding of historical contexts. The researchers used a combination of advanced imaging techniques and AI algorithms to detect and reconstruct the text on the scroll. The project is part of the Vesuvius Challenge, which offers a $1 million prize to incentivize further developments in this technology.

**Background**: The Herculaneum scrolls are ancient Roman documents that were buried and carbonized by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD. These scrolls are extremely fragile and difficult to unroll without causing damage. Advanced imaging techniques, such as X-ray computed tomography (CT) and machine learning, have been developed to read these texts without physically unrolling them.
