FFmpeg 9.1 introduces a new AAC encoder that aims to improve audio quality and address previous issues with chirping artifacts. This improvement in FFmpeg's AAC encoder is significant for audio and video processing, as it addresses long-standing quality issues and provides a better alternative to existing encoders. The new encoder was mainly optimized for 48KHz audio, and it includes workarounds for bugs in the stereo PNS feature of the AAC decoder.

hackernews · ledoge · Jul 1, 14:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747116)

**Background**: Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) is a standard for digital audio compression, designed to be the successor of the MP3 format. It generally achieves higher sound quality than MP3 at the same bit rate. Chirping artifacts are unwanted sounds that can occur in poorly encoded audio files, often described as high-pitched, bird-like noises.

## [IPFS Content Publishing Speed Improved 10x](https://probelab.io/blog/optimistic-provide/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The authors of the article optimized the PUT RPCs and made parts of the process asynchronous, resulting in a 10x faster IPFS content publishing speed. This improvement significantly enhances the efficiency of IPFS, making it more practical for decentralized systems and applications that rely on fast and reliable content distribution. The optimization involves returning control to the user after most of the PUT RPCs have succeeded and continuing the remaining ones in the background. This approach leverages asynchronous processing to reduce latency.

hackernews · dennis-tra · Jul 1, 15:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48748518)

**Background**: IPFS (InterPlanetary File System) is a decentralized file system designed to make the web faster, safer, and more open. It allows users to store and share files in a distributed manner, similar to BitTorrent. The PUT RPCs are used to add content to the IPFS network, and optimizing these processes can greatly improve performance.
